Mission 1

How many solutions? Ask the discriminant

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Move the sliders until the parabola just touches the x-axis at one point.

Watch the number b² − 4ac in the box as you slide. What is it when the curve just touches?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

Solutions of ax² + bx + c = 0 are where the parabola crosses the x-axis. The discriminant tells you how many, without solving:

D = b² − 4ac

D > 0 → two solutions. D = 0 → exactly one. D < 0 → no real solutions.

"Has exactly one solution, find k" means: set D = 0 and solve for k.

Trap: get everything on one side, = 0, first. And keep the sign: in x² − 6x + 9, b is −6.

The equation x² − 6x + k = 0, where k is a constant, has exactly one real solution. What is k?

Mission 2

One solution, none, or infinitely many

≈ 8 min
1

Play

The teal line is fixed. Move the coral line so the two lines never meet.

Then try to make them the same line. What has to match?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

Simplify both sides until it looks like ax + b = cx + d. Then compare:

x-parts differ → one solution

Same x-part, different number → no solution (like 12 = 10, never true). Parallel lines.

Same x-part, same number → infinitely many solutions (both sides identical). Same line.

For a system of two lines: different slopes cross once; same slope, different intercept never meet; same slope and intercept are the same line.

Trap: distribute first. 4(x + 3) is 4x + 12, not 4x + 3.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

In the equation 4(x + 3) = ax + 12, a is a constant. The equation has infinitely many solutions. What is a?

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

A solution of the system is a point on both graphs. To find it:

set the two y's equal → move everything to one side → solve

Each x you get is one crossing. Plug it into the line (easier) to get y.

"How many solutions?" → the discriminant of that combined equation (Mission 1).

Fastest check: type both equations into Desmos and tap where they cross.

Trap: reread what it asks for: x, y, or something like x + y.

Mission 4

Factors and zeros are the same thing

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Make the graph cross the x-axis at −2, 1 and 3.

Watch the factored form in the box. When the graph crosses at −2, what does the factor look like?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

These four statements all mean the same thing:

x = a is a zero ⇔ (x − a) is a factor ⇔ p(a) = 0 ⇔ the graph crosses at a

Is (x − 2) a factor? Plug in 2. If you get 0, yes. If not, the number you get is the remainder when you divide by (x − 2).

Trap: the sign flips. A zero at −2 gives (x + 2). And (2x − 1) gives a zero at x = 1/2, not 1.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

p(x) = x³ − 2x² − 5x + 6. Is (x − 1) a factor of p(x)? What about (x + 1)?

Mission 5

Shifting graphs, exponentials included

≈ 9 min
1

Play

Move the parabola 3 right and 2 down.

Watch the equation. Moving right puts a minus inside the parentheses. Then switch to 2ˣ and move it up: where does it cross the y-axis now?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

y = f(x − h) + k

Outside (+k) moves it up k, the way it looks. Inside works backwards: f(x − 3) moves right 3, f(x + 4) moves left 4.

A point (x, y) on f moves to (x + h, y + k).

Exponentials y = a · bˣ: a is the starting value (the y-intercept). b is what you multiply by each step: b = 1 + r for growth, b = 1 − r for decay. 0.9 means losing 10% each step.

Adding k to an exponential moves the y-intercept to a + k and the flat line it approaches (asymptote) from y = 0 to y = k.

Trap: f(x − 6) is right 6, not left. When unsure, plug in one point and check.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

Six quick ones: shifts first, then exponentials.

Mission 6

Equations with fractions: clear them first

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Equation: 6/x = x − 1. Slide x until both sides are equal.

The left side is the blue curve, the right side is the coral line. Equal sides = where they cross. There are two answers. Can you find both?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

multiply EVERY term by the common denominator

The fractions disappear and it becomes a normal linear or quadratic equation.

One fraction on each side? Cross multiply: a/b = c/d → ad = bc.

Always check: an answer that makes any denominator 0 doesn't count. Throw it out.

Trap: multiplying only the fractions. In x/3 + x/4 = 7, the 7 gets multiplied too.

Part 1: x/3 + x/4 = 7. Part 2: 6/x = x − 1.

Mission 7

Notes → goal: do exactly what it asks

≈ 8 min

A student's notes

  • The axolotl is a salamander from lakes near Mexico City.
  • Unlike most salamanders, it keeps its gills and lives in water its whole life.
  • It can regrow lost legs and parts of its heart and spine.
  • Scientists study it to learn how body tissue regrows.
  • Its close relative, the tiger salamander, loses its gills and moves onto land as an adult.
Goal: contrast the axolotl with the tiger salamander.
1

Play

Every sentence in the box is true. Sort each one: does it do the goal, or not?

That's the whole trick. All four choices on the test match the notes. Only one does the job the question names.

Sort all four sentences.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

read the goal first, skip the notes

1. Read the question. Find the goal: the verb (contrast, emphasize, introduce, explain) and what it's about.

2. Go straight to the choices. Ask one question per choice: does it do THAT? Being true isn't enough.

3. Match the signal: contrast → both things plus "while / unlike / whereas". Similarity → both things plus "both / like". Unfamiliar audience → says what the thing is. Aim of a study → "to find out…".

Trap: the most detailed or most interesting choice. And reading all the notes first wastes a minute you need at the end.

Cheat sheet

Advanced Math

  • How many solutions (quadratic): D = b² − 4ac. Positive 2, zero 1, negative 0. "Exactly one" → set D = 0.
  • Linear: none or infinite. Simplify to ax + b = cx + d. Same x-part + different number → none. Everything the same → infinitely many.
  • Line meets parabola: set the y's equal, everything on one side, solve. Desmos: tap the crossings.
  • Factor ⇔ zero: (x − a) is a factor exactly when p(a) = 0. Zero at −2 → (x + 2).
  • Remainder: dividing by (x − a) leaves p(a).
  • Shifts: f(x − h) + k → right h, up k. Inside works backwards.
  • Exponentials: a · bˣ, a = start (y-intercept), b = 1 + r growth, 1 − r decay.
  • Fractions: multiply every term by the common denominator. Throw out answers that make a denominator 0.

Reading & Writing: notes questions

  • Read the question first. Skip the notes.
  • Underline the goal: contrast, similarity, introduce, emphasize, explain.
  • Every choice is true. Ask: does it do THAT?
  • Contrast → names both things and uses while / unlike / whereas.
  • Unfamiliar audience → says what the thing is.

Test-day pacing

  • Check the clock at question 11 of each math module.
  • Over 90 seconds on one question? Flag it, guess, move on, come back.
  • Never leave a blank. Wrong answers cost nothing.
