Seven missions. Everything the PSAT asks in geometry and trig.

How each mission works · about 8 minutes

  1. Play. Move the figure until you hit the target. Notice what changes.
  2. The rule. Now that you've seen it, here's the rule in one line, plus the trap.
  3. Walk through it. A real PSAT problem, one small step at a time. Each answer unlocks the next step.
  4. Your turn. A new problem, no steps. Get it first try for a star.

Do them in order. Each mission is short on purpose: finish one, take a breath, start the next.

Mission 1

Parallel lines make only two angles

≈ 7 min
1

Play

Tilt the line until a teal angle is exactly 70°.

While you slide, watch all eight angles. How many different sizes do you see?

Not there yet. Keep sliding.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

A line crossing two parallel lines makes 8 angles, but only two sizes: a small one and a big one.

small + big = 180°

For any two angles in the picture, ask one question: same color or different color? Same color → equal. Different colors → add to 180.

Trap: you don't need the names (alternate interior, corresponding…). If one angle looks sharp and the other looks wide, they can't be equal.

Mission 2

Triangle angles: 180° and the outside angle

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Drag the blue dot until the angle at C is 90°.

Watch the big wedge outside corner B. What two colors is it made of?

Not there yet. Keep dragging (arrow keys work too after you tap the dot).
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

A + B + C = 180°

The exterior angle at a corner = the two inside angles that don't touch it. In the figure, the outside wedge at B is literally a coral piece (A) plus a teal piece (C).

Isosceles: two equal angles sit across from two equal sides.

Trap: the inside angle right next to the exterior angle is not part of it. Those two add to 180.

Finish step 2 to unlock.

In triangle ABC, angle A = 50° and angle C = 65°. Side AB is extended past B. Find the exterior angle at B, then figure out which sides are equal.

Mission 3

Pythagoras is about squares

≈ 7 min
1

Play

Right now the legs are 3 and 4, and the long side comes out to exactly 5. Find another pair of legs where the long side is a whole number.

Watch the numbers inside the squares. Add the coral and teal numbers. What do you get?

Try some pairs. (Hint: try doubling 3 and 4.)
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

The square on the long side has the same area as the other two squares combined.

a² + b² = c²

c is the hypotenuse: across from the 90° angle, always the longest side. Only works in right triangles.

Memorize these whole-number sets to skip the math: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25, and multiples like 6-8-10.

Trap: if they give you the hypotenuse and a leg, subtract: b² = c² − a².

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 and one leg of 5. Find the other leg.

Mission 4

SOH CAH TOA depends on where you stand

≈ 13 min
1

Play

Press Stand at B, then move the slider until sin B ≈ 0.80.

Before that, flip between "Stand at A" and "Stand at B" a few times. Which sides change color? Which side never changes?

Not there yet.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

Sin = O/H   Cos = A/H   Tan = O/A

Opposite = the side across from you. Adjacent = the side touching you that isn't the hypotenuse. Hypotenuse never changes.

When you switch corners, opposite and adjacent swap. That's why, when the two small angles add to 90°:

sin A = cos B  ·  sin(x°) = cos(90 − x)°

With a calculator: know an angle and a side? side = 12 × sin(35°). Know two sides and need the angle? Undo it: angle = sin⁻¹(12/13).

Trap: SOH CAH TOA is not on the reference sheet, and tan never uses the hypotenuse. Before any trig on Desmos, check it's in degrees: sin(30) must give 0.5. If it gives −0.988, it's in radians.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

Part 1 (steps 1–5): Right triangle ABC has its right angle at C. AB = 13, BC = 5, AC = 12. Find tan A and cos B.
Part 2 (steps 6–10): the same ideas with a calculator, the way the PSAT usually asks.

Mission 5

Similar triangles: one scale factor

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Slide D until DE = 6. (BC is 12.)

When DE is half of BC, where is D on side AB? Look at the area line too.

Not there yet.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

Same angles → same shape. Every side of the small triangle = big side × k, the scale factor.

A line parallel to one side (DE ∥ BC) always cuts off a smaller similar triangle.

AD/AB = DE/BC = k

lengths ×k   areas ×k²   volumes ×k³

Trap: use whole sides. k is AD/AB, not AD/DB. DB is just the leftover piece.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC with DE ∥ BC. AD = 4, DB = 6, DE = 5. Find BC.

Mission 6

Area and volume: count the cubes

≈ 8 min
1

Play

Build a box with a volume of exactly 24 cubes. Then press Double every side and see what happens to the volume.

There are several boxes that work. Can you find two?

Not there yet.
2

The rule

Finish step 1 to unlock.

Area counts flat squares. Volume counts cubes.

box: V = ℓwh   prism or cylinder: V = (base area) × height

Cylinder base is a circle, so V = πr²h. These formulas are on the reference sheet. Your job is picking the right one and plugging in carefully.

Surface area = add up the area of every face. A cube has 6 equal faces: 6s².

Trap: doubling the sides does not double the volume. Area goes ×4, volume goes ×8.

3

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

A cylinder has radius 3 and height 10. Find its volume. Then see what happens if the radius doubles.

Mission 7

Test-day shortcuts

≈ 9 min
1

Play

Right now the length goes up 20% and the width goes down 20%. Most people guess the area stays the same. Check the readout. Then find a setting where the length goes up and the area changes by exactly 0%.

Not there yet.
2

The shortcuts

Finish step 1 to unlock.

1. Pick easy numbers. When a question has no numbers ("a rectangle's length grows 20%…"), make some up. Use 10 or 100. Then compare before and after.

+20% → ×1.2   −20% → ×0.8   1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96

2. Whole minus part. A shaded region is usually the big shape's area minus the cut-out's area.

3. Similar triangles share trig ratios. Same angles means same sin, cos and tan, no matter the size.

4. Sketch it. No figure given? Draw one and label every number from the question on it before doing any math.

5. Try the answers. Stuck setting up an equation? Plug each answer choice back in and see which one works.

Trap: percent changes don't add. +20% then −20% is not 0%. Multiply the multipliers.

Walk through it

Finish step 2 to unlock.

Three quick problems, one per shortcut: a percent change, a shaded region, and similar triangles.

★

Cheat sheet for the night before

Bluebook's reference sheet gives you

  • Circle: A = πr², C = 2πr
  • Rectangle A = ℓw, triangle A = ½bh
  • a² + b² = c²
  • Special right triangle side ratios
  • Volume: box, cylinder, sphere, cone, pyramid
  • Triangle angles sum to 180°; 360° in a circle

Open it in Bluebook during the test. Practice once so you know where the button is.

You have to carry in your head

  • Parallel lines: same color equal, different colors add to 180
  • Exterior angle = the two far inside angles added
  • Isosceles: equal sides ↔ equal angles across from them
  • Triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25
  • SOH CAH TOA
  • sin x° = cos(90 − x)°
  • Similar: AD/AB = DE/BC; lengths ×k, area ×k², volume ×k³
  • Surface area = add every face
  • Desmos in degrees: sin(30) = 0.5
  • Missing angle: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹
  • Percent changes multiply: ×1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96
  • Shaded = whole − part; no figure → sketch one
SAT bonus

For later: special triangles, arcs, circle equations

Not on the PSAT. College Board lists these for the SAT only. Finish the six missions first. Come back here after the PSAT, or if IB class covers them.

A

Special right triangles

45-45-90 is half a square: x, x, x√2.

30-60-90 is half an equilateral triangle: x, x√3, 2x.

Find the short side x first. Press "Where it comes from" to see the other half.

Trap: in 30-60-90, x√3 is the longer leg, 2x is the hypotenuse.

B

Arcs, sectors, radians

A slice is the fraction θ/360 of the circle.

Arc = fraction × 2πr. Sector = fraction × πr².

180° = π radians. 1 radian is the angle whose arc equals the radius.

C

Circle equations

(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

Center (h, k), radius r. Signs flip, and the right side is r².
